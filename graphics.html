09 — Graphics

Graphic elements

Two signature graphics: the triangle and the rocket. They are how a Plainsight slide announces itself as Plainsight, even before you read the words.

Triangles

Three colours, one motif. Used as section accents, dividers, and edge decorations.

Triangle placement by context

The triangle is a wireframe/outline shape (not filled), drawn with navy, orange, or white stroke lines. Native size is 313 × 358 px. Placement varies by medium.

LinkedIn slide geometry

Triangles appear in pairs at each corner, asymmetrically nested (two same-sized triangles offset from each other). Each triangle is scaled 1.1× from native (344 × 394 px effective). 60% of each triangle is visible; 40% bleeds off the canvas edge.

Top-right pair (Template A only)

  • Back triangle: x = canvas_width − triangle_width + 30
  • Front triangle: shifted left 90 px and down 60 px

Bottom-left pair (both templates)

  • Left triangle (lower): x = −30, bleeds below canvas
  • Right triangle (higher): offset 120 px right from left triangle

Graphic rules

  • One signature per view. A page either has a rocket or a triangle, not both. Two motifs compete.
  • Bleed off the edge. Triangles and rockets work best partially cropped — coming from a corner, exiting at the top — never centered in a clear frame like a photo.
  • Don't recolour. Triangles come in three colours (blue / orange / white) and that's it. Rockets stay in their two-tone palette.
  • Never inside a chart. These are layout decorations, not data marks. A bar chart with a rocket icon for "growth" looks like clip art.
  • Size restraint. A triangle bigger than 1/3 of the page becomes the page. Keep them small — they're accents, not heroes.
  • Triangles are decorative. They must never obscure text or interactive content. On web: always pointer-events: none and aria-hidden="true".
