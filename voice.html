06 — Brand voice

How we sound

We're a Belgian data and AI consultancy. We talk like senior practitioners who respect our reader's time. Plain words, concrete claims, no hype.

Three principles

01

Clear, not clever

Short sentences. Plain English (or plain Dutch). Trade the buzzword for the thing it describes.

02

Concrete, not vague

Numbers, timeframes, named technologies. "30% faster" beats "significantly faster".

03

Confident, not loud

We don't shout in caps or stack exclamation marks. The work speaks; we describe it.

We say / we don't say

Four pairs grounded in our website copy.

We say

We build scalable data platforms and analytics that deliver real ROI.

Direct claim, concrete outcome.

We don't say

We leverage cutting-edge synergies to unlock transformative data journeys.

Buzzwords without a verb that means anything.

We say

Your platform and AI products, 30% faster, using our signature methodology.

Quantified, owned, scoped.

We don't say

Dramatically accelerate your enterprise's AI maturity at scale.

No number, no method, no shape.

We say

A team of data and AI experts passionate about turning complexity into outcomes.

Specific people, specific job.

We don't say

Industry-leading visionaries reshaping the future of intelligence.

Self-praise that says nothing.

We say

Data engineers, analytics engineers, AI engineers — keeping your engine running at peak performance.

Names the roles, names the outcome.

We don't say

Best-in-class talent partners for your digital transformation journey.

Could describe any consultancy on earth.

Language

  • Bilingual by default. Dutch and English are both first-class. Match the audience — Belgian SME landing pages in Dutch, enterprise pitches in English.
  • British / international English over American where it's a choice (organisation, analyse, behaviour).
  • Sentence case for headings. ALL CAPS only for eyebrows, navigation pills and the section labels on the website.
  • One thought per sentence. If you used a semicolon, you probably needed two sentences.
  • No emojis in proposals, contracts or formal client emails. Sparingly on LinkedIn and in internal Slack.
  • Acronyms expanded on first use. "Microsoft Fabric Capacity Units (FCUs)" then "FCUs" thereafter.
