02 — Guidelines

How to use this kit

These guidelines apply to every Plainsight deliverable: pitch decks, proposals, blog posts, dashboards, social posts, internal docs. If you're about to publish something that carries our name, this is where you start.

The six rules

If you remember nothing else, remember these.

  • Blue is the voice, orange is the shout. Plainsight Blue carries roughly 90% of the pigment on any page. Orange is reserved for one thing per view — the primary CTA, the callout number, the single highlighted bar.
  • White (or cream) backgrounds first. Reverse-out blue backgrounds are for the title, the section dividers and the closing slide, not whole decks.
  • One type family. Inter, bold for headings and labels, regular for body copy and quotes. Segoe UI or Calibri where Inter is not installed. No third font.
  • No new hues. If you need more than two colors for a chart, extend with blue tints — never introduce a green, a teal or a purple.
  • The triangle and the rocket are ours. The wireframe triangle is what makes a page recognisably Plainsight before anyone reads a word. Place a corner pair on every cover or title slide, every chapter opener or section divider, and the closing page. Pages that carry the argument stay clean. One motif per piece: triangles or the rocket.
  • Uppercase for labels, sentence case for conclusions. Section headings, eyebrow labels and buttons are uppercase with wider tracking. Action titles, the full sentences that state what a slide or chapter concludes, are in sentence case. Body text is always sentence case.

Never look like AI

Nothing we send to a client should look AI-generated. What gives it away is a default used without a reason: the same shape repeated to fill space, decoration that carries no information, numbers without context. A format is fine when it helps the client understand something; what changes is how we draw it. This applies to proposals, offers, decks, documents, reports, dashboards and artifacts. The website components and the LinkedIn templates are designed systems with their own shapes, and keep them.

Before you use any element, check both: it carries information specific to this client (a real number, a named role, a date, a choice), and it is drawn the Plainsight way, not with a generic default.

  • Never gradients, including gradient text and buttons.
  • Never shadows, glow, glass or blur. Separation comes from a muted cream panel, a hairline or space.
  • Never emoji in client work.
  • Never decorative icons, such as an icon in a tinted circle above every heading.
  • Never rows of identical boxes: three or four cards of the same shape, each with a title and a vague line.
  • Never cards with a coloured left border as the accent.
  • Never check marks, crosses or traffic-light dots standing in for words.
  • Never stock 3D, blobs, isometric people or AI-generated images. Photos are our own people.
  • Never dark slate "tech" themes or rainbow charts. The dark ground is blue; charts are blue with one orange point.
  • Never pill shapes or large rounded corners. Square in decks and print, 8 to 12 px on the web.

Make it look curated

The aim is work that looks made by a design agency for this one client. Editing first, then a few custom moments, then precise finishing. Decoration does not get it there.

  • Summary first. The first screen, slide or paragraph holds the whole story: the decision, the advice in numbers, the key figure with its comparison, and the ask.
  • Argument, then appendix. Five or six chapters at most, each with an action title. Detail tables, assumptions and sources go to an appendix marked A, B, C.
  • One grid, one left edge. Slides use the 5/7 and 2/10 splits on a 1280 × 720 canvas with 64 px margins. Documents put the chapter number in a left margin column.
  • One signature graphic per argument. Look in the visuals catalogue first. Otherwise draw the picture only this client's situation has, to scale.
  • Precise finishing. Numbers and units never break across lines. Equal-width figures in tables. Numbered sources. One number format per piece.

The five-minute checklist

Run through this before you send anything to a client.

  • Is Plainsight Blue (#000075) the dominant color? Body text, headings, primary fills — all blue, not black.
  • Is Orange (#d5693a) used at most for one accent element per view? If everything is orange, nothing is.
  • Is everything set in Inter? Segoe UI or Calibri where Inter is not installed, Arial as the last resort.
  • Is the background white, cream or mist? Dark blue backgrounds only for the title, the dividers and the closing.
  • Have I avoided introducing new hues? Greens, teals, purples — none of them are Plainsight.
  • Does any CTA or highlight have enough contrast? Orange on white needs ≥18 pt, or white on blue. Text on an orange fill is blue.
  • Are the triangles on the cover, the chapter openers and the closing page? Placed from the asset files, never redrawn.
  • Does every element pass the "never look like AI" test? No gradients, shadows, emoji, icon circles, identical card rows or check marks.
  • Are the photos ours? Real Plainsight people from the photo catalogue, untinted, never under text.

When in doubt

Look at plainsight.pro. The website is the canonical reference for how the brand looks in practice. For client work, the manifest (assets/templates/brand.json) wins; when this kit and the website disagree on anything else, please open a ticket so we can fix one of them.
