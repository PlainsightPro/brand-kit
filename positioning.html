Customer pains

The challenges, frustrations and risks that slow data leaders down. These pain points highlight where methodologies, frameworks or skilled expertise may be lacking.

01

Siloed data and integration hurdles

Disparate sources and departmental silos block a unified view. Teams spend excessive time on integration rather than generating value.

02

Poor data quality and trust issues

Inaccurate, inconsistent or incomplete data undermines trust in analytics outputs. Extensive cleansing must happen before any analysis begins.

03

Legacy systems and technical debt

Outdated infrastructure can't keep up with modern analytics needs, resulting in slow performance and project delays.

04

Lack of standardised methodologies

Without proven frameworks, every project is approached ad-hoc, leading to inefficiencies and reinventing the wheel.

05

Talent shortages and skills gaps

45% of CDOs cite talent gaps as a top barrier. Overwhelmed teams and stalled progress are the result.

06

Limited resources vs. high demand

More project requests than capacity. Tight budgets and small teams stretch resources thin and slow delivery.

07

Pressure to deliver quick ROI

Executives expect rapid, tangible returns, putting immense pressure on data leaders to show value before foundations are solid.

08

Unclear role and expectations

The CDO role is relatively new and often poorly defined, leading to overlaps with CIOs and misaligned expectations.

09

Cultural resistance to data-driven decisions

91.9% of organisations cite cultural impediments as the greatest barrier to becoming data-driven. Entrenched habits and scepticism persist.

10

Low data literacy across staff

Business teams lack training to interpret analytics, meaning even the best dashboards go unused without hand-holding.

11

Heavy governance and compliance burdens

Ensuring GDPR/CCPA compliance is resource-intensive and can paint the data function as a bureaucratic cost centre.

12

Difficulty demonstrating ROI

Benefits accrue over time or indirectly, making it hard to justify projects and budgets to sceptical executives.

13

Stakeholder alignment problems

Differing department priorities and KPIs make it a constant challenge to align data initiatives with actual business needs.

14

Rapidly evolving technology landscape

Data leaders must continuously evaluate new tools — risking either chasing every "shiny object" or falling behind competitors.

15

Change management challenges

New data processes meet resistance. Significant effort in training, communication and buy-in slows rollout.

16

Departmental silos in analytics efforts

Different teams build analytics solutions in isolation, duplicating effort and creating multiple versions of the truth.

17

Frequent project stalls or failures

Promising data projects (especially AI/ML pilots) never make it to production, eroding confidence and wasting resources.

18

Short tenure and high turnover in leadership

The average CDO tenure is about 2.5 years. High turnover creates discontinuity in strategy and resets progress.

19

Data security and privacy concerns

Fear of breaches leads to overly restrictive data access policies, slowing analysts and delaying value-driving projects.

20

Analysis paralysis from data overload

Massive data volumes cause teams to over-analyse and hesitate, losing the opportunity for timely data-driven decisions.
