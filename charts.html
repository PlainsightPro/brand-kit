Charts & data visualization

Most Plainsight charts use one colour. The moment a chart uses six different colours, it has stopped being a chart and started being a flag.

Chart palette (ordered)

Use the colors in this order. Don't skip ahead.

#000075
#d5693a
#3939A0
#7373AA
#ADADD4
#A04A20

When to use which

Chart shows…Use colorsExample
One series over time / categories#000075 onlyMonthly revenue, headcount over years
Actual vs. target, us vs. them#000075 + #d5693aForecast vs. actual, before vs. after
3–5 series, no semantic differenceBlue tints in orderRevenue by region
One highlighted item in a list of manyAll gray (Blue 18%) + the one in Orange"This is the bar you should care about"

Chart rules

  • Title in Titillium Web, body in Inter. Same hierarchy as everywhere else.
  • Axes in Blue 55%. Gridlines in Blue 18%. The data is the point — axes should recede.
  • No 3-D, no pie charts of more than 4 slices, no rainbows.
  • Sort bar charts by value unless they're time-ordered. Alphabetical bar charts hide the story.
  • Direct-label where you can. A label on the line is better than a legend two inches away.
  • Round numbers on axes. Forty-two thousand euros is "€42K", not "€42 137.50".

matplotlib / Python

Drop this at the top of any notebook to get on-brand charts by default.

import matplotlib as mpl

PS_BLUE   = "#000075"
PS_ORANGE = "#d5693a"
PS_CYCLE  = ["#000075", "#d5693a", "#3939A0",
             "#7373AA", "#ADADD4", "#A04A20"]

mpl.rcParams.update({
    "font.family":        "Inter",
    "axes.edgecolor":     PS_BLUE,
    "axes.labelcolor":    PS_BLUE,
    "text.color":         PS_BLUE,
    "axes.titlecolor":    PS_BLUE,
    "axes.prop_cycle":    mpl.cycler(color=PS_CYCLE),
    "figure.facecolor":   "white",
    "axes.facecolor":     "white",
    "axes.spines.top":    False,
    "axes.spines.right":  False,
})
