What good Plainsight photography looks like

  • Real people, real moments. Team photos taken at our office, events, client engagements. Faces, not silhouettes.
  • Warm, daylight-led. Natural light, soft contrast, untouched skin tones. No heavy filters, no cool blue grading, no HDR.
  • Workspace context. Whiteboards, laptops, sticky notes, coffee. The visible texture of consultancy work.
  • Medium and wide shots. Close-up portrait crops are reserved for the "Meet the team" page.
  • Diverse and inclusive. Our photography should reflect the team we actually have — and the team we want.

Photo treatments

  • No coloured overlays on photos. We don't tint photos blue or orange to "brand" them — the colour comes from the layout around the photo, not on top of it.
  • The triangle treatment. Where we want a graphic accent, place a thin blue or orange triangle near (not on) the photo — see the website hero and CTA blocks for the pattern.
  • Corner radius: 8–12 px on web, square in print. No circles, no organic blob masks, no decorative borders.
  • Alt text always. Every photo needs descriptive alt text — both for accessibility and for SEO.

Sourcing

Where to find a usable Plainsight-appropriate photo.

Use

  • Team and event photos from plainsight.pro/about
  • Client-supplied photography (with written consent)
  • Editorial stock from Unsplash, Pexels — only when it shows real workspaces
  • Photos from our own events (workshops, Databricks Data Intelligence Day, etc.)

Avoid

  • Stock photos of suited handshakes or pointing at screens
  • AI-generated faces or "people" — they always look slightly wrong
  • Photos with visible competitor logos in the background
  • Anything with a strong blue or orange cast (clashes with our palette)
